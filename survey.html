The physician survey

43 physicians on what makes shadowing work

I surveyed practicing physicians to learn what makes high school shadowing educational, what stops physicians from hosting, and whether a standard structure would help. This page walks through what they said.

What I wanted to know

Six questions behind the survey

  1. What prevents physicians from hosting high school students?
  2. What do physicians believe makes shadowing educational?
  3. Which resources would make hosting easier?
  4. How useful do physicians find standardized shadowing guidelines?
  5. Would physicians be more likely to host if those resources existed?
  6. Do physicians who have hosted see shadowing differently from those who never have?
Who answered

The 43 physicians behind the numbers

The survey was cross-sectional and completed online. Most respondents practiced internal medicine, and 85% worked in a field that involves procedures. Nearly half had practiced for five to ten years.

What the data shows

Five findings

Physicians came across as willing. The friction sits in time, structure, and unclear rules.

1. Most physicians would host, given support

More than half of the sample chose the highest rating on both scales. Nobody rated standardized guidelines a 1.

2. Time leads the barriers, and structure follows close behind

Four of the five most common barriers concern rules and structure, which a well-designed program could address.

3. Physicians who have never hosted worry about different things

Never-hosts were far more likely to name hospital policies and student preparedness. Experienced hosts were more likely to name scheduling. Uncertainty seems to keep physicians out, while logistics wear on the ones already in.

4. Guidelines matter most to the physicians outside the system

Physicians who rated standardized guidelines as more useful also reported being more likely to host. Among never-hosts that link was strong. Among experienced hosts it nearly vanished. Both groups rated the guidelines about equally useful (4.13 and 4.33 out of 5), yet never-hosts were a full point less likely to host (3.50 against 4.56).

5. Physicians want prepared students more than a fixed schedule

Lining up each barrier with the resource meant to solve it shows where physicians put their weight. Preparation and clear rules were requested far more often than a structured schedule or privacy training.

  • Barrier named51%Hospital or institutional policies

Reading these results carefully

What this survey cannot tell us

These results are exploratory. They are strong enough to guide a program and too small to settle a question.

  • Small sample

    43 physicians is enough to describe patterns and too few to generalize to all physicians.

  • Weighted toward internal medicine

    24 of 43 respondents practiced internal medicine, and several specialties had three or fewer.

  • Who chose to answer

    Physicians willing to fill out a survey about shadowing may already care more about teaching students.

  • Stated intentions

    Saying you would host a student is different from hosting one.

  • One point in time

    A cross-sectional survey measures beliefs on one day and cannot establish cause.

  • Checkbox limits

    Some questions asked for up to three choices, and the form did not enforce the limit, so those counts are descriptive.
